# SIDE B product marketing context

## Product

SIDE B is an editorial guide to Tokyo places where music is a meaningful
reason to visit. It combines the point of view of an independent culture
magazine with the utility of a location-aware product.

## Audience and job

The primary audience is someone deciding where to spend a music-led night in
Tokyo. They want a small, trusted selection and enough detail about sound,
mood, neighborhood, and price to choose a room without opening many tabs.

The MVP journey is: browse the guide or map, notice a venue, save it, and
eventually visit. The primary current action is viewing all seven venues on
the map; saving a venue is the secondary action.

## Positioning

- Core promise: choose your next Tokyo music bar by sound and atmosphere, not
  only by proximity or rating.
- Differentiator: human-edited observations about selection, sound systems,
  record collections, and whether the room suits a particular kind of night.
- Product vision: “Discover places worth listening to.”

## Voice

Clear before clever. Editorial, observant, warm, and understated. The copy
may feel like a Japanese city magazine, but it should not make the reader
decode decorative language. Headlines can be expressive; body copy and
actions should be direct. Avoid hype, exclamation marks, generic travel copy,
and vinyl clichés.

Japanese is the default language and should read as original Japanese rather
than a translation. English should be compact and conversational.

## Claims and proof limits

The current seven venues, details, map positions, and images are fictional
prototype data. Never imply that they are real, verified, currently open, or
recommended from firsthand reporting. Keep the prototype notice prominent.
Production copy must distinguish verified facts, community observations, and
SIDE B editorial interpretation.
